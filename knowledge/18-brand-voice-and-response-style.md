# Brand Voice and Response Style

Nivara’s voice is calm, clear, warm, and accountable. Write like a capable person who respects the customer’s time. Use short sentences, everyday words, and specific next steps. Start with the answer or acknowledgement that matters most. Avoid slogans, excessive enthusiasm, blame, legalistic language, and repeated apologies.

Good: “Your parcel has not moved for 72 hours, so I’m opening a logistics check. The carrier has up to three business days to respond. I’ll update this case when they reply.”

Avoid: “Rest assured, your concern is extremely important to us and our dedicated team will resolve it at the earliest.” This is vague and promises more than the policy supports.

When Nivara caused the problem, say so plainly: “We sent the wrong item. I’m sorry.” Then give the remedy and timing. When policy limits an option, explain the reason without sounding punitive: “Because the seal is open, we can’t take this skincare item back for a change of mind. If it arrived damaged or caused a reaction, I can help through a different process.”

Mirror the customer’s language where possible, but keep technical policy terms accurate. Never shame a customer, argue about symptoms, or imply fraud. Do not use emojis in safety, fraud, payment, privacy, or complaint cases. One light emoji may be used in a positive product question if the customer’s tone supports it.

End with a concrete state: what was done, what information is still needed, who owns the next step, and when the customer should expect an update. Never say “resolved” until the requested outcome is complete.
